# GHOST OPS: SECOND STRIKE

## Oneshot — _Operation PALE HORSE_

_A Rainbow Six–flavoured, three-act oneshot for the Fudge dice core rules._ _(D6/D100 conversion note at the very end if your table prefers those.)_

---

## 1. Handler's Overview

**Premise:** A brand-new joint counter-terror unit, **Task Force VECTOR**, is scrambled to a chemical attack in a small European town. The attack is strange — the gas isn't very lethal. Two months later, the UN flags an outbreak of a horrifyingly lethal virus in the Middle East. VECTOR realises the "failed" chemical attack was a live-fire dry run for a delivery system, and the real weapon is about to go operational. They have **three days** to find and neutralise the lab before it moves to an unknown next location.

**Structure (aim for a 3–4 hour session):**

|Act|Name|Content|Time budget|
|---|---|---|---|
|1|First Contact|Combat raid + interrogation/notebook choice|45–60 min|
|—|Bridge|One paragraph of narration, no dice|2 min|
|2|Silent Vector|Investigation clock — find the district, then the building|60–90 min|
|3|Pale Horse|Storm the lab, stop Dr. Kessler|60–90 min|

**Tone:** Clancy-thriller cold open, procedural investigation middle, hard breach finale. Keep Act 1 fast and violent — it exists to teach the combat rules and plant the hook. Keep Act 2 tense but not punishing — the clock should make players sweat, not force a TPK. Act 3 is the payoff: let it be loud.

A note on real-world grounding: the outbreak numbers are frightening by design, but this is a straight bioterror-thriller premise, not a real disease. Keep it in "movie plausible" territory and move past any medical detail quickly — the game doesn't need epidemiology, it needs a ticking clock.

## 3. ACT 1 — "First Contact"

### Read-aloud briefing

> _"Six hours ago, a chemical device was triggered in the market square of Löwenstadt — a small town about forty minutes outside Strasbourg. Local first responders are down or fled. We've got a confirmed hostile cell still on-site, armed, holding the square. VECTOR is first qualified asset on the ground — you are wheels-down in eleven minutes. Your objective: neutralise the cell, secure the area, and get eyes on what that gas actually was. Command wants answers, not just bodies."_

### The scene

Löwenstadt's market square: overturned stalls, an abandoned delivery van rigged as the dispersal point, low-visibility haze still clearing. Civilians are down but — notably, once anyone checks (Medical or First Aid roll, DN 5) — **not dying**. Symptoms read as disorientation, nausea, short-term blindness. Not what a lethal chemical attack looks like.

**Tangos — Cell "Little Falcon":**

|Type|Count|Awareness|Skill|Ranged|Melee|Wounds|Defence|Morale|
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
|Minor (lookouts, AK47)|4|2|2|3|2|3|1|—|
|Standard (gas-masked gunmen, AK47)|3|3|3|4|3|4|2|3|
|**Major — Milo Veseli (cell leader)**|1|4|3|5|4|6|3|4|

Veseli carries a sidearm, a sealed **notebook** (coded logistics notes), and — if taken alive — knows more than he wants to admit.

Run it as a standard Battle Breakdown (initiative → actions → resolve, core rules pg. 67–69). Minors break and flee once two of their number drop; Standards hold until Veseli is down or the players roll well above his Morale DN (2 + 4 = 6).

### The choice

Once the square is secure, give the players the branch directly:

- **Interrogate Veseli** (must be captured alive, non-lethal takedown or surrender). Contested roll: Intellect + HUMINT/Investigation vs. Veseli's Intellect-based resistance (DN 6). Success: he cracks and names "the Vesalius Network" and mentions a second, "real" device — but he doesn't know the target, only that it's "already moving."
- **Recover and decode the notebook** (found on his body or dropped in the firefight — Perception or a search action to find it, DN 4). Requires a follow-up Intellect + SIGINT/Investigation roll (DN 6, Cipher's specialty) to break the encoding. Success reveals shipping manifests referencing a **second production site** and, critically, buys the squad **+2 days** on the Act 2 clock, because the team goes into Act 2 already knowing what they're looking for instead of starting cold.

Both are viable; only the notebook grants the time bonus, which is the point — a real trade-off, not a trap.

**If the players get neither** (Veseli dies, notebook destroyed): that's fine. Act 2 simply starts on the _tighter_ clock. Don't punish the table further than the currency you already built for this.

## 4. ACT 2 — "Silent Vector"

### Read-aloud briefing

> _"Six days ago, the WHO flagged a cluster of deaths in Marwan, [fictional nation of Al-Sarid] — a haemorrhagic virus, lethality north of sixty percent, contained fast because it burned through its hosts too quickly to spread far. That containment is the only reason you're getting this briefing instead of reading about a pandemic. Analysts believe this is a field test — the real device. Signals intelligence suggests the production site relocates roughly every three days to avoid exactly this kind of response. You have until then. Find the lab. Shut it down."_

If the team has the +2-day bonus from Act 1, say so explicitly here — five days instead of three.

### The Clock

Represent the deadline as **6 Time Blocks** (12 hours each — 3 days), or **10 Time Blocks** with the bonus. Track it visibly (a drawn-out bar, tokens, whatever your table likes).

Every investigative action below **costs 1 Time Block**, success or failure. This is the tension engine of the act — it doesn't matter how skilled the squad is, time is still the enemy.

### Phase A — Which District?

Marwan has four candidate districts: **Downtown, Factory, Train Station, Market**. (Handler: privately decide now — suggest **Factory District**, it pays off cleanly into Act 3's layout below — and don't reveal it.)

Each specialisation below points at leads. Players choose which to pursue each Time Block; more than one operator can work in parallel (each spends their own Time Block, working the clock down faster but covering more ground):

|Specialist|Action|Roll|DN|
|---|---|---|---|
|Hound (HUMINT)|Lean on informants in the districts|Intellect + HUMINT|6|
|Cipher (SIGINT)|Intercept local comms/chatter|Intellect + SIGINT|6|
|Cipher (Drone)|Fly recon over traffic/supply patterns|Focus + Drone Operator|5|
|Hound (K9)|Kajtek works a chemical-residue sample from Löwenstadt against local air/soil|Focus + K9 Training|6|

**On a success:** the Handler gives one accurate detail pointing toward Factory District (a supplier's odd shipping pattern, a chemical smell reported near the tanneries, a comms burst routed through an industrial relay). Collect **two independent successes pointing the same direction** and the district is confirmed.

**On a failure:** the lead points convincingly toward a _wrong_ district, or goes cold and just burns the Time Block. If the players act on a false lead without cross-checking, trigger a **Complication** (below) rather than simply wasting more time — false confidence should cost something visible, not just a shrug.

### Phase B — Which Building?

Once Factory District is confirmed, repeat the same action list, but successes now narrow a shortlist of buildings (a disused chemical plant, a cold-storage warehouse, a rail-freight depot) down to the **disused chemical plant** — thematically clean, since it's already zoned for hazardous material and won't draw inspection.

Two successes (any combination of specialists) confirms the target. If the clock runs out before confirmation, don't hard-fail — see below.

### Complications (roll or choose, d6)

|d6|Complication|
|---|---|
|1|A Vesalius sleeper spots the team asking questions — a tail must be lost (Stealth/Driving contest) or the network goes dark, adding +1 required success to the current phase|
|2|Local police detain an operator over the false lead — costs an extra Time Block to extract them|
|3|A source is willing to talk but wants something (extraction, cash, protection) — a short roleplay beat, then the lead resolves as normal|
|4|Counter-surveillance drone/watcher spotted — Cipher can suppress it (Computers, DN 6) or it burns a Time Block regardless|
|5|A genuine but unconfirmed tip about a _civilian_ casualty risk at the target — raises Act 3 stakes (see hostages, below)|
|6|Good luck — this action's next roll gets Advantage|

### If the clock runs out

Don't stop the game. The squad breaches with imperfect intel: apply one or two of the following in Act 3 rather than ending the mission —

- No layout intel: the map below is revealed room-by-room instead of known in advance.
- No warning of the guard rotation: the first room's Tangos get a free round of action before initiative is rolled.
- The Handler narrates that Command is now watching a live satellite feed and the pressure is explicit — good for tone, costs nothing mechanically.

The mission is still winnable. The clock's job is dread, not a coin flip on TPK.

## 5. ACT 3 — "Pale Horse"

### Read-aloud briefing

> _"Confirmed: sub-level lab beneath the old Marwan Chemical Works. Thermal shows at least a dozen hostiles and active refrigeration — consistent with live cultures. We believe Dr. Elias Kessler, the Vesalius Network's chief scientist, is on-site personally overseeing final packaging for shipment. If that shipment leaves the building, we lose it. You are weapons-free. Go loud, go now."_

### The Lab — modular layout

**Zone 1 — Loading Dock / Guard Post.** Entry point. Roller doors, a guard hut, stacked chemical drums (cover). First contact happens here.

**Zone 2 — Production Floor.** Open industrial space converted with fermentation tanks and packaging lines. Civilians here are **conscripted lab techs, not willing combatants** — if the "civilian risk" complication triggered in Act 2, add 2–3 of them as hostages/bystanders who need to be cleared safely, not shot through.

**Zone 3 — Containment & Server Room.** Sealed behind a hazmat door (Coordination + CBRNE Training or a Breaching roll, DN 6, to open safely; forcing it without the roll exposes anyone within Short range to a chemical hazard — treat as 4 damage, armour does not reduce this, Hazmat Suit or Gas Mask negates it entirely). This is where Kessler and the finished product are.

**The failsafe:** if Kessler reaches the server room console unimpeded (2 full rounds of being left alone), he triggers a **destructive purge** — not a bioweapon release, but an incineration protocol meant to destroy evidence and the lab itself. This should read as an escape/stop -the-clock action-movie beat: operators now have a countdown (3 rounds) to grab Kessler and any recoverable samples and get clear before the building goes up. It's a good, cinematic way to end the mission whether or not it goes perfectly.

### Tangos — Vesalius Lab Security

|Type|Count|Awareness|Skill|Ranged|Melee|Wounds|Defence|Morale|
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
|Minor (perimeter guards, AK47)|4–6|2|2|3|2|3|1|—|
|Standard (interior security, AR15/vests)|3|3|3|4|3|4|2|3|
|**Major — Foreman Aldric Voss (security chief)**|1|4|3|5|4|6|3|4|

**HVT — Dr. Elias Kessler (Untouchable):** Awareness 4, Skill 5, Ranged 4, Melee 3, Wounds 12, Defence 4, Morale 5. Unarmed or lightly armed — his value is in capture, information, and the failsafe threat, not a stand-up fight. If cornered without an escape route he'll surrender rather than trigger the purge on top of himself; if he has a clear exit, he'll run for it and trigger the purge behind him.

### Ending the session

- **Full success** (lab cleared, Kessler captured, samples secured): VECTOR just stopped a global outbreak and stands up a real intelligence win against the Vesalius Network — good hook for a sequel.
- **Partial success** (lab destroyed, Kessler dead or escaped, no samples): the immediate threat is over, but the Network survives to come back — also a good hook, darker tone.
- **Rough night** (heavy casualties, civilians lost, Kessler escapes clean): still not a failure state worth punishing hard — this is a oneshot; let the debrief carry the weight narratively rather than mechanically.

## Appendix — Quick Reference

**Core DN guide:** Body shot 4, Head shot 6, Arms 6, Legs 5. Cover adds Disadvantage per size. Aiming (spend an action) grants Advantage per action spent, lost if not resolved same round.

**Armour ratings used above:** Kevlar Vest AR3 (ENC2), Plate Carrier X-SAPI AR4 (ENC3), IOTV MK3 AR5 (ENC3).

**Morale:** base DN 2 + Tango's Morale stat; roll when a side loses roughly a third of its numbers.

**Boosts/Busts:** each Boost spent by a player hands the Handler a Bust — use Busts sparingly to twist a roll or throw a complication in, never to make a challenge outright impossible.
